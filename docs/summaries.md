# Plain-language summaries

Implementation: `core/sessions` (`Summaries.kt`, `ReasonTexts.kt`), `SummaryPolicy` version `0.1.0`.
Issue #26. Targets come from reference profiles ([reference-profiles.md](reference-profiles.md)).

The Session and Compare screens start with a short summary in words; the existing numbers stay below,
behind *Show the numbers*. The texts are generated by fixed, versioned rules: offline, deterministic
(the same data gives the same text), and exported with the results (`summaries` in
[export-format.md](export-format.md)), so a shared export shows what the user saw. A summary only
describes; it never changes a value, a quality gate or a comparison verdict.

## Screens

* **Session screen**: a compact "In plain words" card with one sentence per position
  (`PositionSummary.brief`, e.g. "Seat A: about 0.7 s in the speech range, 0.13 s above the target. 5 of 5
  claps usable.") and a *See the details* button. The recordings stay under each position as before.
* **Session summary screen** (opened from that card): one card per position with the headline, the shape, the
  bands that could not be measured, the **band diagram** (a bar per octave band with its value; a dashed
  outline and "not measured" where there is no value) and the recording quality; then the **target scale**
  per position (a scale from 0 s with the target zone shaded and the position marked, drawn only for an
  evaluated position), the hints and the notes.
* **Compare screen**: the headline, then one row per position with the speech-range change and a
  **before/after diagram** (paired bars per octave band; a band without enough usable, non-invalid claps has
  no bar), with *Show every band and attempt* on the row for the plot and every number.

The diagrams draw only what the texts already say (`SummaryBand`, `TargetItem`, `ChangeBand` in the
export), never a value for an invalid band, and carry a spoken description for accessibility.

## Wording rules

* No statistical-confidence language: never "significant", "certain", "confidence" or a percentage of
  confidence. Changes are "more than the normal variation" or "Change is inconclusive".
* Levels are never called dB SPL (the summaries mention no levels at all).
* T20 may be called "reverberation" or "how long sound takes to die away"; EDT never appears, and
  nothing replaces T20.
* An invalid band gives a reason, never a number, even when a stored record carries a stale fit.
  Questionable bands are named and keep their value.
* Built-in targets are "UK school guidance for … (BB93, Table 6)", never "the standard for your home";
  user targets are "your target"; imported ones carry the declared citation and "(imported)". A
  whole-room target compared with one position is "a guide, not a pass or fail".
* The app knows nothing about the room's layout, so no text assumes windows, furniture or walls.
* Hints say what *usually* helps, never what will happen.
* Until the REW comparison (#3) and repeatability (#5) are done, every summary ends with "Not yet checked
  against reference equipment."

`SummariesTest` checks every rule and a wording test runs all fixtures through the rules and fails on
"significan", "confidence", "certain", "dB SPL", "EDT", "probab", "proven", "guarantee", or a value of
an invalid band.

## Session summary (per position)

Only impulse attempts analysed with the app's current algorithm version are used.

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Band value | median T20 of the usable claps; only with at least `minUsableAttempts` = 3 usable claps (the comparison's minimum), otherwise "not measured" with the most common invalid reason |
| Headline | "After a clap, sound takes about *X* s to die away in the speech range at *position*", X = mean of the 500, 1000 and 2000 Hz values (one decimal); if one is missing, which band and why |
| Shape | high long: mean(2000, 4000) ≥ `highLongRatio` (1.15) × mean(500, 1000); mid/low long: mean(250, 500, 1000) ≥ `midLowLongRatio` (1.15) × mean(2000, 4000); otherwise even |
| Quality | "*n* of *N* claps usable in the speech range" (a value in all three speech bands), the most common problem among the others, and its advice text (the same texts the Attempt screen shows) |
| Target | the profile's evaluation of the position's band values: above / at / below and by how much (two decimals), or why it can't be evaluated |

## Hints (room level)

From the median across positions of each band's value:

| Condition | Hint |
|---|---|
| every measured position at or below the target | say so, and that a ringing echo may be a flutter echo the claps can't show (soft furnishings or absorbers on one of the two walls usually help); no band-shape or area suggestions |
| high tones long | soft furnishings: curtains, rug, upholstered chairs, fabric wall hangings |
| mid/low tones long | thin materials barely help; thick porous absorbers (5–10 cm, ideally with an air gap), sofa, full bookshelves |
| even, and above the target | more absorbing area in general |
| longest position ≥ `positionsDifferRatio` (1.25) × shortest | start near the longest |
| 250 Hz measured nowhere | "can't judge low tones from claps" |
| volume and target set, above the target | Sabine estimate (below) |
| always | claps can't show everything (e.g. flutter echo between parallel bare walls) |

### Sabine estimate

Sabine's reverberation formula, T = k·V/A, rearranged for the extra equivalent absorption area that
would bring the measured speech-range average T_now (mean over the measured positions) to the target
limit T_target:

ΔA = k · V · (1/T_target − 1/T_now)  m² of fully absorbing surface

shown rounded ("roughly 3 m²", to 0.5 m² below 10 m²) and phrased as an estimate; real materials absorb
only part of the sound, so more of them is needed. No material absorption coefficients are shipped.

**k is derived, not typed in.** A diffuse field decays 60 dB (a factor 10⁶ in energy) in
T = 6·ln(10)·4V / (c·A), so k = 24·ln(10)/c; with c = 343 m/s (air at about 20 °C) k ≈ 0.161 s/m
(`Sabine.constant`). The formula is public-domain physics (W. C. Sabine, *Collected Papers on
Acoustics*, Harvard University Press, 1922). **Not yet verified against that text:** the public copies
(archive.org, Project Gutenberg, HathiTrust) are not reachable from the development sandbox. Until it is
checked, the estimate relies on the derivation above, which anyone can recompute.

## Comparison summary

Built only from the verdicts the comparison policy produced ([dsp.md §8](dsp.md#8-repeats-and-comparisons)):

* **Per position**: down (or up) by more than the normal variation only when all three speech-band cells
  have that verdict, with the size of the speech-range change; "Change is inconclusive" otherwise (naming
  any single band that exceeded); "not enough usable claps" when a speech cell has too few.
* **Where**: the bands whose verdict exceeded, largest change first; "biggest change in the high (mid)
  tones" when their mean |change| is ≥ `changeShapeRatio` (1.5) × the other's, with "typical for soft
  furnishings such as curtains and rugs" (or "for thick absorbers") only when the intervention text names
  one *and* the pattern fits.
* **Normal variation**: the largest V of the speech cells, explained as the larger of the clap spread and
  the baseline/repeat difference.
* **Next steps**: no repeat baseline → record one; differing capture configurations → which, and record
  again; too few claps → where; older analyses → re-analyse.
* **Target**: the treatment's speech-range average against the room's target.
